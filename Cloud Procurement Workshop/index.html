Understanding cloud for procurement

Cloud means your data and applications run on someone else's servers, over the internet. You pay for what you use. Many of us already use cloud services — email, document storage, or collaboration tools.

Cloud can give you flexibility to scale up or down, stronger security, and often lower cost because you only pay for what you use.

How cloud is delivered

Public cloud

You share infrastructure with other organisations. Providers include Amazon, Google and Microsoft. Anyone can sign up and start using it.

Private cloud

The infrastructure serves your organisation only. You get more control. It can sit in your own data centre or be hosted elsewhere.

Hybrid cloud

A mix of public, private and on-premise systems. Keep sensitive data on private cloud. Use public cloud for general workloads.

What you can buy (service types)

From basic infrastructure to ready-made applications:

IaaS — Infrastructure as a Service

You get servers, storage and networking. You manage the operating system, applications and data. Pay for what you use.

Examples: AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud. Use for web hosting, app development, databases and disaster recovery.

PaaS — Platform as a Service

The provider runs the infrastructure and platform. You build and run your applications on top. You focus on code.

Examples: Heroku, Google App Engine. Use for building web apps, mobile apps and automation.

SaaS — Software as a Service

Ready-to-use applications over the internet. You subscribe. Minimal setup — this is the most common type for councils.

Examples: Office 365, Google Workspace, Salesforce. Use for email, collaboration, CRM and project management.

FaaS — Function as a Service (serverless)

Run code without managing servers. It runs when triggered — for example when someone uploads a file or calls an API.

Examples: AWS Lambda, Azure Functions. Use for image processing, data pipelines and small automated tasks.

Welsh Procurement Policy Statement (WPPS) — 10 principles

The WPPS sets expectations on public bodies (including local authorities) for how to procure. The principles apply to procuring organisations and can be used to assess how well those bodies are complying with Welsh procurement policy. They are not supplier assessment criteria, but they influence how procurements are designed (e.g. incorporating community benefits or sustainability). In the evaluation, score how well this procurement (your approach) reflects each principle (1 = weak, 4 = strong).

  1. Collaborative procurement — Maximise long-term sustainable social and economic value through collaborative procurement in Wales.
  2. Policy integration — Integrate procurement into Welsh policy development and implementation.
  3. Sustainable procurement — Progress long-term sustainable procurement, best practice, and delivery of organisational well-being objectives.
  4. Procurement profession — Raise the standing and profile of the profession as an enabler for procurement policy.
  5. Progressive procurement — Support Foundational and Circular Economy, place-based activity, resilient local supply chains.
  6. Climate action — Prioritise carbon reduction and zero emissions; net zero public sector Wales by 2030.
  7. Stakeholder involvement — Align ways of working and increase stakeholder involvement for innovative and sustainable solutions.
  8. Fair Work — Promote equal opportunities and Fair Work in Wales (ethical employment, supply chains).
  9. Digital solutions — Improve integration and user experience of digital solutions; maximise use of procurement data for decision making.
  10. Value-based procurement — Deliver optimum long-term outcomes for Wales (whole-life value, outcomes).

3. How will you buy it? (route to market)

Choose the route that best fits your needs. All routes use pre-approved suppliers so you buy compliantly.

Framework

Pre-approved suppliers. Direct award or further competition. Open and closed frameworks under Procurement Act 2023 (opens in new tab).

Pros: Established. Standard terms. Cons: Closed frameworks — no new suppliers after award.

Dynamic Purchasing System (DPS)

Suppliers can join at any time. Buy through a DPS (opens in new tab).

Pros: New suppliers add competition. Flexible. Cons: No direct award. Further competition only.

Dynamic market

New under Procurement Act 2023. Market stays open. Buy through a dynamic market (opens in new tab).

Pros: Innovation. New suppliers join anytime. Cons: Above-threshold only. More steps than catalogue.

Catalogue

Simple purchases. Order online for quick delivery. CCS catalogues (opens in new tab).

Pros: Fast. Fixed prices. Cons: Simple items only. Not for complex cloud services.

For cloud: G-Cloud (Digital Marketplace), Sell2Wales and CDP. CCS How to buy (opens in new tab)
